\begin{abstract}
    The ratio of $s$-process to $\alpha$-element enrichment ($[s/\alpha]$) correlates with stellar age and has been proposed as a chemical clock. However, the $[s/\alpha]$--age relation varies with metallicity and location in the Galaxy, and the underlying mechanism is not well understood. We quantify the age--[Ce/Mg] correlation across the Galactic disk in over 100,000 red giant stars in the Milky Way Mapper survey. 
    We find that the slope of the correlation varies significantly with metallicity and guiding radius in the chemical thin disk. The trend is steepest in the outer disk and below Solar metallicity, while the most metal-rich stars and those in the inner disk show no correlation with age. Halo stars have higher [Ce/Mg] than the chemical thick disk, suggesting that AGB enrichment is important even at low metallicity.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Stellar ages are a vital component of Galactic archaeology studies. Stellar age data provide direct measurements of the Galaxy's evolution over time and allow for better comparisons with chemical evolution models. Recent years have seen an explosion of large stellar catalogs with age measurements for $10^5-10^6$ stars \citep[e.g.,][]{nataf_accurate_2024,stone-martinez_starflow_2025,wang_spectroscopic_2025,roberts_cn_2026}. However, most age estimation techniques are observationally expensive or apply to a limited range of stellar parameters. ``Chemical clocks,'' or empirically-calibrated relations between stellar age and atmospheric abundance ratios, promise reliable spectroscopic age estimates over a wider parameter space. 
% The best-studied chemical clock is [C/N], which has a strong foundation in stellar physics but can only be used for stars on the red giant branch (RGB).

A number of studies have proposed the use of $[s/\alpha]$\textemdash the ratio of elements produced by the slow neutron-capture process to the $\alpha$-elements produced in core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe)\textemdash as a chemical clock. A remarkably tight correlation between [Y/Mg] and age was found in solar twins \citep{nissen_high-precision_2015} and red clump stars \citep{slumstrup_ymg_2017}, and \citet{nissen_high-precision_2015,nissen_high-precision_2016} first proposed using [Y/Mg] as a chemical clock.
% \citet{nissen_high-precision_2015} reported a tight correlation between [Y/Mg] and isochronal age for solar twins and proposed its use as a sensitive chronometer. \citet{slumstrup_ymg_2017} also found this correlation in solar-metallicity red clump stars. 
More recently, \citet{casali_tracing_2025} found that this correlation extends to several $s$-process and $\alpha$-elements, in particular [Ce/Mg]. After calibrating on metallicity and asteroseismic age, \citet{casali_tracing_2025} used [Ce/Mg] to estimate ages for the full APOGEE survey.

The basic mechanism of the $[s/\alpha]$ chemical clock is the difference in the enrichment timescales of the two elements. The progenitors of CCSNe are short-lived, so the $\alpha$-element abundances closely trace the star formation history. The $s$-process elements are primarily produced in the asymptotic giant branch (AGB) phase of low- to intermediate-mass stars \citep[$\sim1-10\Msun$; see review by][]{karakas_dawes_2014} whose main-sequence lifetimes range from $\sim30\Myr-10\Gyr$. In lower-mass ($\lesssim4\Msun$) stars, the neutron source is the \ce{^13C(\alpha,$n$)^16O} reaction, which requires partial mixing from the convective envelope into the He-rich intershell region. The formation of the \ce{^13C} pocket is not well understood, and in nucleosynthetic models it is typically incorporated artificially and tuned to reproduce observations \citep{karakas_dawes_2014}.

However, there is evidence that the $[s/\alpha]$--age relation is not universal. \citet{feltzing_metallicity_2017} found that the [Y/Mg]--age relation depends strongly on metallicity in the Solar neighborhood, a finding that has been confirmed for multiple elements \citep[e.g.,][]{casali_gaia-eso_2020,sales-silva_exploring_2022}. \citet{casali_gaia-eso_2020} found that open clusters in the inner disk have lower [Y/Mg] than their outer-disk counterparts of similar age. \citet{sales-silva_exploring_2022} found that the [Ce/$\alpha$]--age relation in open clusters varies across the disk. \citet{ratcliffe_chemical_2024} found that the age--$[s/\alpha]$ relation varies with the guiding-center radius and birth radius of the star. \citet{casali_tracing_2025} quantified the metallicity dependence to fit an age--[Ce/Mg]--[Fe/H] relation, which they applied to the full APOGEE survey to estimate ages. \todo{More discussion of environmental dependence and implications for the chemical clock. We then explore possible mechanisms for the relation, such as modifications to the star formation history and yield prescription, in multizone GCE models. In Section \ref{sec:data}, we describe our observational dataset. In Section \ref{sec:results}, we present the chemical abundance trends in our sample. In Section \ref{sec:gce}, we compare our results with chemical evolution model predictions. In Section \ref{sec:conclusions}, we summarize our conclusions.

We use data from the Milky Way Mapper (MWM; J.A. Johnson, in preparation), a program of the fifth generation of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey \citep[SDSS-V;][]{kollmeier_sdss-v_2017,kollmeier_sloan_2025} data release 19 \citep[DR19;][]{sdss_collaboration_nineteenth_2025}. The catalog includes all data from the previous Apache Point Observatory Galactic Evolution Experiment (APOGEE) DR17 \citep{abdurrouf_seventeenth_2022}. MWM DR19 data were obtained from the infrared APOGEE spectrographs \citep{wilson_apache_2019} mounted on the 2.5-meter Sloan Foundation Telescope \citep{gunn_25_2006} at Apache Point Observatory and the Ir{\'e}n{\'e}e du Pont Telescope \citep{bowen_optical_1973} at Las Campanas Observatory. 

Atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances were determined with the APOGEE Stellar Parameter and Chemical Abundance Pipeline \citep[ASPCAP;][]{garcia_perez_aspcap_2016}, which compares a grid of synthetic spectra \citep{hubeny_tlusty_2021} to the observed spectra using $\chi^2$ minimization. The atomic and molecular line lists are presented in \citet{hasselquist_identification_2016}, \citet{cunha_adding_2017}, and \citet{smith_apogee_2021}. 
Cerium abundances specifically are derived using Ce II lines present in the APOGEE spectral windows. \citet{cunha_adding_2017} identified eight such lines, but only three are used by ASPCAP for abundance determination in DR19: \num{15784.75}, \num{16376.48}, and $\SI{16595.18}{\angstrom}$. All DR19 abundances assume local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE). The spectral synthesis is further described by \citet{holtzman_apogee_2018} and \citet{jonsson_apogee_2020}.

\subsection{Kinematics}

The 6-D kinematic properties for stars in our sample are calculated using position, proper motion, parallax, and radial velocity measurements from {\it Gaia} DR3 \citep{gaia_collaboration_gaia_2023}. We adopt distance estimates from \citet{zhang_parameters_2023}, the {\it Gaia} parallax, and \citet{bailer-jones_estimating_2021}, taking the first finite value from these sources in the order listed. These coordinates are transformed into the Galactocentric cylindrical frame $(R,\phi,z)_{\rm gal}$ with {\tt astropy} \citep{astropy_collaboration_astropy_2013,astropy_collaboration_astropy_2018,astropy_collaboration_astropy_2022} using updated solar position and motion parameters from \citet{hunt_multiple_2022}. In particular, we adopt the Galactic position of the Sun $(R,z)_\odot=(8275,20.8)\pc$ \citep{gravity_collaboration_improved_2021,bennett_vertical_2019} and solar velocity with respect to the Galactic center $(v_x,v_y,v_z)_\odot=(8.4,251.8,8.4)\kms$ \citep{reid_proper_2020,gravity_collaboration_improved_2021}. Orbital parameters such as the angular momentum $L_z$, energy $E$, guiding-center radius $R_g$, and maximum midplane distance $z_{\rm max}$ are calculated using the {\tt gala} package \citep{price-whelan_gala_2017} with the {\tt MilkyWayPotential2022} potential.

\subsection{Stellar Ages}

We use the StarFlow \citep{stone-martinez_starflow_2025} catalog of stellar ages for MWM DR19. \citet{stone-martinez_starflow_2025} use a deep generative model trained on asteroseismic data to estimate ages for evolved stars. Their model retains information about the parameter space distribution of the training data and accounts for asymmetric uncertainties. Stars with $\bracket{Fe}<-1.2$ are excluded from their catalog due to the sparse sampling of asteroseismic data at low metallicity. We adopt their recommended training space density threshold of $>3\times10^9$, resulting in estimated ages for \todo{104,710} stars with $\bracket{Fe}\gtrsim-1.0$. The remaining \todo{$\sim$18,000} stars have typically lower metallicity, including most stars with $\bracket{Fe}<-0.8$. The left-hand panel of Figure \ref{fig:kiel-diagram} compares the Kiel diagram of this stellar age sample against our full sample.

The ``good age'' sample serves as the primary sample for this work. However, the age quality cut introduces a bias against low-metallicity stars. This effect is minor for disk stars, but it eliminates nearly all halo stars. For cases where we calibrate abundance trends as a function of metallicity (e.g., Sections \ref{sec:logg-corrections} and \ref{sec:galactic-trends}) or investigate the halo (Section \ref{sec:halo}), we instead use the full sample defined in Section \ref{sec:data}.

\subsection{Low- and High-Ia Populations}

Milky Way disk stars can be divided into two populations based on their $[\alpha/{\rm Fe}]$ ratios, often termed low- and high-$\alpha$ or the chemical thin and thick disks. We refer to these populations as high- and low-Ia, respectively \citep[adopting the nomenclature of, e.g.,][]{griffith_abundance_2019}, out of recognition that the former population is enriched in Fe by SNe Ia relative to the latter.
We divide our sample into high-Ia and low-Ia populations using the following boundary condition:
\begin{equation}
    \bracket[Mg]{Fe} = 
    \begin{cases}
        -0.09, & \bracket{Fe}>0 \\
        -0.09+0.13\times\bracket{Fe}, & \bracket{Fe}<0.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:alpha-cut}
\end{equation}
High-Ia stars lie above this boundary, while low-Ia stars lie below; we exclude stars within $\pm0.02\dex$ of this boundary from either population.

The ASPCAP parameters for giant stars in DR19 have a precision of $50-90$ K for $T_{\rm eff}$, $0.07-0.09\dex$ for $\log(g)$, and $0.02-0.04\dex$ for [M/H], [$\alpha$/M], and [Mg/H] \citep{meszaros_sdss-v_2025}. Due to its weaker lines, [Ce/H] is less precisely measured, with a median reported uncertainty  of $\sigma_{\bracket{Ce}}\approx0.047\dex$ for our sample. \citet{meszaros_sdss-v_2025} measured the scatter in [Ce/M] for open clusters, wide binaries, and the solar neighborhood, and estimated a precision of $\sim0.23\dex$ across all three methods. ASPCAP-derived abundance errors are known to be underestimated \citep[see][]{pinsonneault_apokasc-3_2025}, and the factor $\sim5$ difference between the reported and externally-calibrated precision is comparable to other elements in DR19 \citep{meszaros_sdss-v_2025}.

Figure \ref{fig:ce-lines-1} presents the spectra for 6 ``sibling'' stars with similar $\log(g)$, $T_{\rm eff}$, and [Fe/H] that span a range of [Ce/Fe]. All of the stars have $S/N\sim200$, close to the median $S/N$ of the full sample. Each of the three spectral windows used for Ce abundance determination are shown in the figure. The relative Ce enrichment is reflected in the difference between the observed flux and the global fit, which accounts for stellar parameters such as [M/H], $\log(g)$, and $T_{\rm eff}$, but not individual abundances. The [Ce/H] best-fit model is computed by re-scaling the model [M/H] to the individual element abundance in each window, and therefore matches the data most closely near each of the the Ce II lines. The [Ce/H] fit produces a closer match to the data than the global fit for all of the selected spectra.

Figure \ref{fig:ce-lines-2} similarly presents the spectra for 6 selected stars spanning a range of $\log(g)$, $T_{\rm eff}$, and [Fe/H]. The top three spectra span a range of $\log(g)$ at similar [Fe/H] and [Ce/H], illustrating that the Ce II lines get fainter with increasing surface gravity but are still detectable at $\log(g)\approx3$. The bottom three spectra span a range of metallicities at similar $\log(g)$. The lines are weak at $\bracket{Fe}\approx-1$ and strongly blended at $\bracket{Fe}\approx+0.3$, but are still fit well by the model at both extremes. Overall, Figures \ref{fig:ce-lines-1} and \ref{fig:ce-lines-2} demonstrate that the APOGEE spectra can accurately capture variation in [Ce/H] across the full parameter space in the sample.

Some likely artificial structures are visible in low-density regions of the abundance space. We note the presence of a series of ``ridges'' in the [Ce/Mg]--[Mg/H] abundance distribution between $-1.0\lesssim\bracket[Mg]{Ce}\lesssim-0.4$ that we speculate are due to lower-quality fits clustering near grid lines. \citet{meszaros_sdss-v_2025} noted a similar noding behavior for some stars with $\bracket{M}>0$ and $\bracket[M]{Ce}\approx-0.2$. This behavior is most noticeable for stars with $\log(g)>3.0$, where the Ce lines are weaker. These artifacts affect a small minority of stars in our sample \todo{(what percent at most?)}, so while we make note of them here, we do not take further steps to remedy them. We have tested removing all stars with $\log(g)>2.5$ from our analysis and found minimal differences in our results.

ASPCAP does not include a treatment for upper limits, so the reported abundances may in some cases be theoretically unmeasurable. By comparing a grid of synthetic spectra with and without each element, \citet{shetrone_apache_2025} calibrated a function to determine an upper limit on the abundance for a given $T_{\rm eff}$ and $S/N$. For most of the stars in our sample, the measured [Ce/H] is above this limit due to our $S/N>100$ requirement. However, at lower metallicities, a larger fraction of our sample falls below these upper limits, which affects our analysis of the Milky Way halo in Section \ref{sec:halo}. Therefore, we calculate upper limits on [Ce/H] for each star using the method of \citet{shetrone_apache_2025} and eliminate \todo{589} stars with abundances below the limit.

The DR19 abundances include a zero-point offset $\Delta_{\rm X}$ to force the solar neighborhood, solar metallicity sample to have a mean of $\bracket{X}=0$ for each element \citep{meszaros_sdss-v_2025}. While $\Delta_{\rm X}$ is small for many elements (e.g, $\Delta_{\rm Mg}=+0.004\dex$ and $\Delta_{\rm Fe}=+0.014\dex$), the zero-point offset for Ce is fairly large, $\Delta_{\rm Ce}=+0.149\dex$. This is an important consideration when comparing DR19 abundance trends to other catalogs or previous data releases of SDSS.

Stellar abundance trends should be independent of stellar parameters, in particular surface gravity. Stars on the lower RGB, red clump, and upper RGB should have similar abundance trends at the population level. However, because surface gravity has a significant effect on the strength of spectral lines (see Figure \ref{fig:ce-lines-2}), abundance measurements can show spurious correlations with $\log(g)$. Figure \ref{fig:logg-calibrations} shows that the median trends in un-corrected [X/Mg] as a function of [Mg/H] vary with $\log(g)$. For Fe, this variation is at the level of $<0.05\dex$, but the Ce trends vary by as much as $\sim0.3\dex$. We therefore apply $\log(g)$-corrective offsets to the DR19 abundances.

We follow the procedure outlined in \citet{sit_chemical_2024} to remove $\log(g)$ trends in Fe and Ce. Following \citet{sit_chemical_2024}, we define a calibration sample of stars in DR19 with $-0.75 < \bracket{Mg} < 0.45$, $3<R_{\rm gal}<13\kpc$, and $|z| < 2\kpc$, in addition to the cuts listed in Section \ref{sec:data}. In brief, corrective offsets are applied to each individual [Fe/H] and [Ce/H] such that the median [X/Mg] trend with [Mg/H] is the same over all $\log(g)$ in the Galactic disk. [X/Mg] medians are calculated in $0.1\dex$-wide bins of [Mg/H] and $0.25\dex$-wide bins of $\log(g)$. Differences in the median values of each bin from a fiducial value (selected to be $\bracket{Mg} = 0$ and $\log(g) = 1.75$) are calculated separately for the low-Ia and high-Ia sequences. Then a number-weighted average of median differences over the two alpha sequences is calculated at each ([Mg/H], $\log(g)$) bin pair. The final offset value for a given star is calculated by interpolating the binned median differences to that star's [Mg/H] and $\log(g)$. We finally subtract a zero-point offset equal to the median difference between the corrected and un-corrected abundances for the entire sample, to avoid shifting the overall distribution of [Ce/H] and [Fe/H].

Figure \ref{fig:logg-calibrations} shows the effect of the corrective offsets on [Ce/Mg] and [Fe/Mg]. The corrective offsets reduce variation in the Ce trends to the $\sim0.1\dex$ level, ensuring that abundances are consistent across the full range of $\log(g)$ in the sample. We see similar levels of improvement when applying the analysis in Figure \ref{fig:logg-calibrations} to specific regions of the Galaxy (e.g., inner disk, solar neighborhood, and outer disk). We have tested running our analysis on the un-corrected abundances and found minimal differences in our results. We have also compared our approach to that of \citet{meszaros_sdss-v_2025}, who correct for trends in $T_{\rm eff}$ within open clusters. While both approaches reduce abundance correlations with $T_{\rm eff}$, our method better reduces the correlations with $\log(g)$, allowing us to more accurately compare abundance trends across the Galactic disk. Throughout the remainder of this paper, we will use these corrected abundances unless otherwise noted.

\subsection{Age Trend Validation}

\begin{figure}
    \centering

Each survey uses different age estimation techniques, but the abundances on the y-axis all come from MWM DR19. The solid curves plot the running median [Ce/Mg] as a function of age for each data set, and the dashed curves plot the StarFlow running median for comparison.     \label{fig:dataset-comparison}
\end{figure}

We first validate the StarFlow ages against other age catalogs for DR19. Figure \ref{fig:dataset-comparison} compares the age--[Ce/Mg] relation from our sample against two other age catalogs: the APOGEE-Kepler Asteroseismic Catalog \citep[APOKASC-3;][]{pinsonneault_apokasc-3_2025} and the Open Cluster Chemical Abundances and Mapping Survey \citep[OCCAM;][]{otto_open_2026}. Although APOKASC-3 uses DR17 stellar parameters and abundances, in Figure \ref{fig:dataset-comparison} we plot the DR19 abundances due to the difference in zero-point offsets between the data releases. 

The median trend in our sample matches the APOKASC-3 and OCCAM trends well. Agreement with APOKASC-3 is expected because StarFlow uses training data from APOKASC-3 \citep{stone-martinez_starflow_2025}. Some of the differences can be attributed to a difference in survey coverage: APOKASC-3 stars lie predominantly in the solar circle, whereas our sample covers a much wider area of the disk. 

The OCCAM cluster ages are more informative because they are fully independent. The OCCAM data include clusters with at least one RGB star with a measurement of [Ce/H]. Most of the clusters are young, $\lesssim2\Gyr$, and these clusters also show the largest spread in [Ce/Mg]; some have [Ce/Mg] ratios $\sim0.3\dex$ higher than our median trend. The OCCAM age--[Ce/Mg] trend agrees with our sample up to ages of $\sim7\Gyr$. Overall, this suggests that the StarFlow ages are suitable for studying broad age--abundance trends, though perhaps not yet for addressing finer details in the shape of the relation, such as possible breaks or subtle curvature.

\subsection{Metallicity Dependence}
\label{sec:metallicity-trends}

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/cemg_mgh_age.pdf}
    \caption{{\it Top:} The distribution of abundances in the [Ce/Mg]--[Mg/H] plane for our full sample, color-coded by the median stellar age. Each hexagonal bin contains a minimum of 10 stars; in lower-density regions, each star is plotted individually. The grayscale outlines represent linearly-spaced density contours, with the black contour encompassing the highest density of stars. The error bars in the upper right corner represent the median uncertainty in [Ce/Mg] and [Mg/H]. {\it Bottom:} Similar, but showing the median stellar [Fe/H] as a function of [Ce/Mg] and age.}
    \label{fig:cemg-mgh-age}
\end{figure}

We first explore the global age--metallicity--[Ce/Mg] relation for our full sample. The top panel of Figure \ref{fig:cemg-mgh-age} shows the median stellar age as a function of [Mg/H] and [Ce/Mg]. A broad age trend is immediately apparent: stars with low [Ce/Mg] are on average the oldest in the sample, while stars with high [Ce/Mg] are the youngest. Stars with solar [Ce/Mg] have median ages of $\sim4-6\Gyr$, across a wide range of metallicity ($-0.5\lesssim\bracket{Mg}\lesssim+0.2$). The spread in [Ce/Mg] and age is broadest at $\bracket{Mg}\approx-0.2$, with most of the old, low-[Ce/Mg] stars belonging to the low-Ia population. The most Mg-rich stars occupy a narrower range of near-solar [Ce/Mg] with median ages of $\sim6-7\Gyr$, and some of the abundance bins with $\bracket[Mg]{Ce}>0$ are older on average than their low-[Ce/Mg] counterparts. 

The bottom panel of Figure \ref{fig:cemg-mgh-age} shows that the youngest stars span a broad range of [Ce/Mg] values, and that the median metallicity is inversely proportional to their [Ce/Mg]. For old stars, this trend is flipped, and stars with high [Ce/Mg] have higher metallicities on average than stars with lower [Ce/Mg]. Figure \ref{fig:cemg-mgh-age} provides the first hints that the global age--[Ce/Mg] trend has an additional dependence on metallicity.

\begin{figure*}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/local_metallicity_fits.pdf}
    \caption{The variation in the age--[Ce/Mg] relation as a function of metallicity in the solar neighborhood ($7\leq R_g<9\kpc$, $z_{\rm max}<0.5\kpc$). {\it Left:} Each panel compares the stars within the given metallicity bin (colored points) against the full solar neighborhood sample (gray points). The solid line shows the linear regression to the data in that metallicity bin, and the dashed line shows the extrapolated relation outside the fitted age range. Open points represent low-Ia stars, which are not used in the fit. The $\odot$ symbol marks the location of the Sun. {\it Right:} The best-fit parameters in the solar neighborhood as a function of metallicity. The dashed line represents the \citet{casali_tracing_2025} age--[Ce/Mg]--[Fe/H] relation.}
    \label{fig:local-metallicity-trends}
\end{figure*}

To quantify the metallicity dependence in the solar neighborhood, we fit a linear trend to the data in bins of metallicity.
% Figure \ref{fig:local-metallicity-trends} shows how the age--[Ce/Mg] relation varies as a function of metallicity in the solar neighborhood. 
In detail, bin the stars by [Fe/H] with a width of $0.1\dex$ and fit a linear least-squares regression in each bin. We only fit stars between $1-8\Gyr$ of age; outside this range, the StarFlow ages diverge from the asteroseismic test sample \citep[Figure 5,][]{stone-martinez_starflow_2025} and [C/N]-derived ages are generally less accurate \citep{roberts_cn_2026}. We also limit our fits to high-Ia stars because the low-Ia population has less accurate ages and shows different trends with [Mg/H] and $R_g$ (see Section \ref{sec:galactic-trends}). To reduce the degeneracy between the regression slope and intercept, we fit the intercept at age $\tau=5\Gyr$, closer to the middle of the sample.

Both the slope and intercept of the fits vary with metallicity, as shown in Figure \ref{fig:local-metallicity-trends}. Metal-rich stars show shallow or flat slopes, with intercepts at or below Solar [Ce/Mg] at $\tau=5\Gyr$. Stars with slightly sub-solar metallicity ($-0.3\leq\bracket{M}\leq0.0$) have the steepest trends and the highest [Ce/Mg] at $\tau=5\Gyr$. This contrasts with the approach of \citet{casali_tracing_2025}, who allow only the intercept to vary with [Fe/H] when fitting a trend to their {\it Kepler} field stars. \todo{Explain Casali study in more detail, possibly in introduction.} We note that even in the optimal range of [Fe/H], with a slope of $-0.05\dex\kpc^{-1}$, the reported median [Ce/H] uncertainty of $0.05\dex$ places a floor of $1\Gyr$ on the precision of [Ce/Mg]-derived ages using MWM abundances. The situation is worse for metal-rich stars, where the flat age--[Ce/Mg] trend renders the chemical clock essentially useless.

Our fitting method is deliberately simple to illustrate the broad variation with metallicity. We caution that the large and asymmetric age uncertainties reported by \citet{stone-martinez_starflow_2025} could result in a systematically shallower slope than the ``true'' relation. Additionally, the Ce abundance uncertainties in MWM are likely underestimated, potentially by up to a factor of 4 (see discussion in Section \ref{sec:ce-abundances}). A complete fit to the local age--[Ce/Mg]--[Fe/H] relation should account for these effects.

\subsection{Trends across the Galaxy}
\label{sec:galactic-trends}

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/global_metallicity_fits.pdf}
    \caption{Best-fit parameters for the linear regression to the age--[Ce/Mg] relation as a function of metallicity and guiding radius. All fits are restricted to high-Ia stars with $z_{\rm max}<0.5\kpc$. The shaded bands indicate the uncertainty in the best-fit parameters at each metallicity bin. The red curve plots the relation in the solar neighborhood and is the same as Figure \ref{fig:local-metallicity-trends}. {\it Top:} The slope as a function of metallicity. The dotted gray line indicates a flat slope. {\it Bottom:} The intercept at $\tau=5\Gyr$ as a function of metallicity. The $\odot$ symbol marks the Sun's location.}
    \label{fig:global-metallicity-fits}
\end{figure}

We repeat the analysis of Section \ref{sec:metallicity-trends} in multiple $2\kpc$-wide radial bins between $R_g=3-15\kpc$. Figure \ref{fig:global-metallicity-fits} plots the slope and intercept at $\tau=5\Gyr$ as a function of [Fe/H] for each of the radial bins. If the metallicity variation was universal, Figure \ref{fig:global-metallicity-fits} would show similar behavior in each of the $R_g$ bins. We observe similar patterns in some regimes, but not others. Metal-rich stars ($\bracket{Fe}\ge+0.2$) have shallow or flat trends across the disk, with similar slopes and intercepts. Metal-poor stars ($\bracket{Fe}<-0.2$) also have slopes that agree within uncertainties, although the slope uncertainties are large due to smaller sample sizes and shorter age baselines in these bins. Near solar metallicity, however, the trends diverge: inner-disk stars have steeper slopes than their outer-disk counterparts. At $\bracket{Fe}=-0.2$, the value at $\tau=5\Gyr$ differs by $\sim0.3\dex$ between the inner-most and outer-most radial bins.

\begin{figure*}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/median_trends_grid.pdf}
    \caption{The median [Ce/Mg] as a function of [Mg/H] for high- and low-Ia populations across the Galaxy. Each panel corresponds to a bin in $R_{\rm guide}$ (increasing left to right) and $z_{\rm max}$ (increasing bottom to top). The small blue (red) points plot individual abundances of high-Ia (low-Ia) stars. For visual clarity, only a random sample of 1,000 stars are plotted in each panel. The number of high- and low-Ia stars in each region is indicated in the top-right corner of each panel. The solid lines with large points plot the median [Ce/Mg] in bins of [Mg/H] for each region, and for reference the dashed lines indicate the median trends in the Solar neighborhood ($7\leq R_{\rm guide}<9\kpc$, $z_{\rm max}<0.5\kpc$). The gray error bars in the top left panel indicate the median abundance errors for the full DR19 sample.}
    \label{fig:median-trends-grid}
\end{figure*}

We next investigate positional variation in the [Ce/Mg]--[Mg/H] relation directly, removing stellar age from the equation.
Figure \ref{fig:median-trends-grid} shows how the [Ce/Mg] median trend with [Mg/H] changes with position in the Galaxy. In the solar neighborhood, the median [Ce/Mg] of high-Ia stars peaks at $\bracket{Mg}=-0.2$ and is lowest for the most metal-rich stars. The location of the trend maximum is consistent in most regions of the Galaxy, but the maximum [Ce/Mg] is lowest at small $R_g$ and highest at large $R_g$.  This behavior is consistent with [Ce/Mg] as a chemical clock: in the inside-out formation scenario of the Milky Way, the inner Galaxy formed first and is therefore dominated by older stars, while the outer Galaxy formed more recently \citep{bird_inside_2021}. Interestingly, the most metal-rich stars in each region have slightly sub-solar [Ce/Mg] ratios, consistent with the solar neighborhood trend. These stars are most likely migrators from inner regions, populating the metal-rich tail of the distribution in each region, so their similar [Ce/Mg] ratios suggest similar birth radii.

At greater distances from the midplane, the high-Ia trends are flatter and have lower [Ce/Mg] on average, leading to less separation between the high- and low-Ia sequences. One explanation is that stars with higher $z_{\rm max}$ have experienced more vertical heating and are therefore older, so they have lower [Ce/Mg] on average. Alternatively, these stars could have formed out of a vertically heated gas disk, in which case their trends indicate an additional environmental dependence for the chemical clock. Both mechanisms could be at play in the high-$z_{\rm max}$ population. Because age--velocity trends are difficult to interpret away from the midplane \todo{(cite)}, we limit most of our analysis to stars with $z_{\rm max}<0.5\kpc$.

The low-Ia trend, by contrast, is remarkably consistent in each region: roughly constant at $\bracket[Mg]{Ce}\approx-0.2$ below $\bracket{Mg}<0$, and increasing up to $\bracket[Mg]{Ce}\approx0$ at higher metallicity. While the high-Ia trend in most regions is concave-down, the low-Ia trend is concave-up, most notably at high latitudes. The metal-rich tail of the high-Ia and low-Ia populations show very similar [Ce/Mg] ratios, and we note that the true agreement may be closer than shown in Figure \ref{fig:median-trends-grid} because by definition the populations are separated by $\ge0.04\dex$ in [Fe/Mg] (Equation \ref{eq:alpha-cut}). The low-Ia abundance patterns are consistent with the picture that this population formed rapidly and in a well-mixed environment in the inner Galaxy, with radial migration contributing low-Ia stars to the solar neighborhood and outer regions \todo{(references)}.

Overall, Figures \ref{fig:global-metallicity-fits} and \ref{fig:median-trends-grid} complicate the picture of [Ce/Mg] as a chemical clock. Broadly, old populations have low [Ce/Mg] and young populations have high [Ce/Mg], but there are also clear trends with metallicity and location in the Galaxy. These effects are also hard to disentangle: the highest [Ce/Mg] ratios are found in the outer Galaxy, where stellar populations are young {\it and} low-metallicity. These results imply that a chemical clock calibrated in the solar neighborhood, even accounting for the metallicity dependence, will fail when applied in other regions of the Galaxy.

To disentangle the evolution of Ce from that of Mg, we define a residual abundance from the median trend with [Mg/H]. For each star, we calculate the residual abundance
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\bracket{Ce} \equiv \bracket{Ce} - {\rm med}(\bracket{Ce} | \bracket{Mg}),
\end{equation}
with the median trend computed separately for the low- and high-Ia populations. This is a simplified version of the residual abundance analysis of \citet{weinberg_chemical_2022}. Figure \ref{fig:residual-explainer} illustrates the residual abundance calculation for the Solar neighborhood. We found in Section \ref{sec:galactic-trends} that abundance trends vary with position in the Galaxy, so we compute the median high-Ia trend separately for each region presented in Figure \ref{fig:median-trends-grid}: five $2\kpc$-wide bins in $R_g$ between $3-13\kpc$, and three bins in $z_{\rm max}$ corresponding to $0-0.5\kpc$, $0.5-1\kpc$, and $1-2\kpc$. For low-Ia stars, we use the global median trend because variations between regions are small and some regions have few low-Ia stars (see Figure \ref{fig:median-trends-grid}).

\begin{figure}

Figure \ref{fig:gradients} shows the radial [Ce/H] and [Ce/Mg] gradients in bins of stellar age. We define our halo sample by the following cut in the $E-L_z$ plane:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{E_{\rm halo}}{\SI{e5}{\kilo\meter\squared\per\second\squared}} > -0.7 - \exp\Big(\frac{L_z}{\SI{1.5e3}{\kilo\parsec\kilo\meter\per\second}}\Big).
    \label{eq:halo-cut}
\end{equation}
\citet{hawkins_using_2015} proposed using [Mn/Mg] and [Al/Fe] to distinguish stars accreted from dwarf galaxies from stars that were born in the disk or proto-Milky Way. We split our halo sample into accreted and {\it in situ} populations using the following chemical cut:
\begin{equation}
    \bracket[Mg]{Mn}_{\rm acc.} <
    \begin{cases}
        -0.2, & \bracket[Fe]{Al}<-0.2; \\
        -0.6-2\times\bracket[Fe]{Al}, & \bracket[Fe]{Al}\ge-0.2.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:accreted-cut}
\end{equation}
We do not apply the corrective $\log(g)$ abundance offsets to the halo sample because many of the stars fall outside the metallicity range of the calibration sample.

Figure \ref{fig:halo} plots [Ce/Mg] as a function of [Mg/H] for the halo stars as compared to the Milky Way disk. \subsection{Summary}

We confirm the global correlation between [Ce/Mg] and age in the MWM DR19 dataset. We find that this relation depends significantly on metallicity, and even after correcting for this dependence we still observe variation in the trend across the Galaxy. Similarly, a decomposition of the radial Ce gradient with age reveals varying trends with radius and non-monotonic evolution between the low-Ia, old high-Ia, and young high-Ia populations. Halo stars behave non-monatonically with metallicity and have [Ce/Mg] ratios in between the low- and high-Ia populations. Overall, the data suggest that the [Ce/Mg] is influenced by both the star formation history and the physics of AGB nucleosynthesis, complicating its use as a chemical clock.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The slope of the age--[Ce/Mg] relation for high-Ia stars varies substantially with metallicity. Stars with $-0.3\leq\bracket{Fe}\leq0.0$ have the steepest trend, whereas stars with $\bracket{Fe}\ge+0.3$ have a flat trend. Theoretical AGB yields predict a maximum Ce enrichment at $\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)\approx-0.3$, which aligns with the observed behavior.
    \item We also observe substantial variation in the age--[Ce/Mg] trend with guiding radius. The trend is steepest in the outer disk, at $R_g\ge13\kpc$, and flat in the inner disk, at $R_g\le5\kpc$. After calculating a residual Ce abundance to account for the effect of metallicity, the radial trends persist, suggesting that the age--[Ce/Mg] relation is influenced by the local star formation history.
    \item Low-Ia stars have sub-Solar [Ce/Mg] ratios, and show consistent abundance patterns across the disk. In contrast, low-metallicity halo stars have $\bracket[Mg]{Ce}\approx0$, and this is consistent between accreted and {\it in situ} populations. The relatively high [Ce/Mg] of halo stars suggests that AGB enrichment is important even at $\bracket{Mg}\sim-1$.
    % \item A chemical evolution model of the disk is unable to reproduce the age--[Ce/Mg] relation with standard AGB yield prescriptions. After shifting the production of Ce to lower-mass AGB stars to increase the enrichment timescale, our model qualitatively reproduces the age--[Ce/Mg] trends across the high-Ia disk. However, our model fails to reproduce the abundances of low-Ia and halo stars, so more work is needed to understand the chemical evolution of the $s$-process.
\end{itemize}
